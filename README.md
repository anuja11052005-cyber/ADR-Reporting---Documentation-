# ADR Reporting & Documentation – Virtual Work Lab

## Task 2

### Title
ADR Reporting & Documentation

### Objective
To complete a structured Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR) reporting form based on a given case scenario and demonstrate the basic process of pharmacovigilance reporting.

## ADR Reporting Process

1. Identify the suspected adverse drug reaction.
2. Document patient information.
3. Document the suspected medication.
4. Record the adverse reaction and timeline.
5. Record relevant medical history and concomitant medications.
6. Assess seriousness and outcome.
7. Perform a basic causality assessment.
8. Submit and document the ADR report.



### Case 1 – Amoxicillin

- Patient: 25-year-old male
- Indication: Bacterial throat infection
- Suspected drug: Amoxicillin
- Dose: 500 mg
- Adverse reaction: Skin rash and itching
- Time to onset: 3 days after starting treatment
- Suspected ADR: Possible allergic skin reaction
- Dechallenge: Symptoms improved after discontinuation
- Seriousness: Not reported as serious
- Causality: Possible

### Case 2 – Metformin

- Patient: 45-year-old female
- Indication: Type 2 diabetes mellitus
- Suspected drug: Metformin
- Adverse reaction: Nausea, abdominal discomfort and diarrhea
- Time to onset: During the first week
- Suspected ADR: Gastrointestinal adverse effects
- Seriousness: Not reported as serious
- Causality: Possible
Conclusion

The cases demonstrate the identification and documentation of suspected adverse drug reactions. Proper ADR reporting is an important component of pharmacovigilance because it helps in monitoring the safety of medicines.

 Skills Demonstrated
